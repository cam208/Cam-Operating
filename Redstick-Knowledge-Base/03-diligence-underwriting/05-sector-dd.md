# Sector Diligence — Food Productivity

The diligence that generalist funds can't do well and Redstick can. This is where the
operator edge (Cam on food operations, Shane on manufacturing/supply chain) turns into
lower loss rates. Run alongside the technical DD (Stage 5) and feed it into the Market,
Technology, and Unit-Economics lenses.

## The food-productivity DD checklist

### 1. The buyer & the sale (Market lens)
- **Who signs the check** in the food value chain — grower, processor, distributor,
  grocer, restaurant operator, or consumer? Name the exact ICP, not "the food industry."
- **Willingness to pay** — food runs on thin margins. Does the productivity gain show up
  in *their* P&L fast enough to justify the spend? Quantify the payback period.
- **Sales cycle reality** — enterprise food buyers (grocery chains, large processors)
  are slow and procurement-heavy. Does the GTM match the buyer's actual buying behavior?
- **Bottoms-up TAM only.** Reject top-down "1% of the $X trillion food market." Build it
  from named customer segments × realistic spend. In the market stress test, check the
  **software-only** TAM — flag if the founder's number bundles consulting/labor savings.

### 2. Unit economics (Unit-Economics lens — path to >60% gross margin)
- **Hardware vs software margin structure** — robotics/hardware carries COGS, service,
  and deployment costs that software doesn't. Model the real gross margin, not the
  aspirational SaaS one.
- **Deployment & support cost** — food environments are harsh (wet, cold, abrasive,
  regulated). Installation, maintenance, and uptime cost real money per site.
- **CAC vs contract value** — long food sales cycles inflate CAC; make sure LTV supports
  it.
- **Payback for the customer** — restate the customer's ROI: labor hours saved, yield
  gained, waste avoided, downtime reduced — in dollars.

### 3. Supply chain & manufacturing (Shane's lens — for any hardware/bio company)
- **Can it actually be built at scale?** BOM, contract manufacturing, lead times, single-
  source component risk.
- **Field reliability** — MTBF in a real plant/farm, not a lab. Uptime is the product for
  food robotics.
- **Serviceability** — who fixes it when it breaks at 4am in a processing plant?
- **Perishability & food-safety fit** — does the system respect cold chain, sanitation,
  and contamination constraints?

### 4. Technology & defensibility (Technology lens)
- **Demo vs deployment** — is there a working system in a real food environment, or a lab
  prototype? Deck description alone caps the Tech score at 2.
- **Defensibility** — proprietary data, IP/patents, hardware+software integration, or
  workflow lock-in? Or a thin wrapper on a third-party model?
- **Platform dependency** — is the company 100% reliant on a single third-party API/model
  where a pricing or policy change alters unit economics overnight? Flag as a warning
  (counts once — don't double-count in the stress test).

### 5. Regulatory & timeline (kill-criteria screen)
- **Time to launch** — any regulatory blocker preventing launch for **> 18 months** is a
  hard kill for Fund I.
- **Food-safety / agency approvals** — for bio/materials and anything entering the food
  stream, map the approval path and who owns it.
- **Enforcement reality** — in the market stress test, verify that any deadline or
  regulation the thesis relies on is actually holding, not slipping.

## Vector-specific DD emphasis

| Vector | Diligence weight lands on |
|---|---|
| **Labour Productivity (Robotics)** | Field reliability, deployment cost, payback vs labor cost, manufacturing scale |
| **Optimization (CV/Sensing)** | Accuracy in real conditions, integration into existing lines, data moat |
| **Decision Automation (AI/Ops)** | Data access, workflow lock-in, platform dependency, ROI attribution |
| **Input Efficiency (Bio/Materials)** | Regulatory path (>18mo = kill), scale-up/manufacturing, unit cost at volume |

## Portfolio-concentration check

Before advancing, check the deal against current Fund I holdings for sub-vector overlap
and rate concentration **Low / Medium / High** (this feeds the triage record and
[`../04-portfolio-construction/02-concentration-limits.md`](../04-portfolio-construction/02-concentration-limits.md)).
Five near-identical produce-grading CV companies is correlated risk, not diversification.

## Strategic-LP fit (record it during DD)

Note any factors relevant to policy-capital LP reporting (FCC/CFIN): Canadian founders,
female founders, Indigenous founders, clean-tech angle, regional economic development.
Not a scoring input — but it matters for LP relations
([`../06-lp-relations-fund-ops/`](../06-lp-relations-fund-ops/)) and is captured on every
triage.
