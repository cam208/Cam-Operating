# 08.01 — Positioning

The definition of what Redstick is, who it is for, and why a founder or an LP picks it
over the alternatives. This is the source file for every public-facing sentence about
the firm. When the deck, the site, the newsletter, or a pitch drifts from this
document, correct the asset — not this file (unless a fund fact in
[`../../CLAUDE.md`](../../CLAUDE.md) changed, in which case update both).

> Canonical facts live in [`../../CLAUDE.md`](../../CLAUDE.md). Fund facts here are

## 1. The statements (use verbatim)

**One-line:**
> Redstick Ventures backs the AI and robotics making food more productive, from farm
> to consumer.

**Elevator (≈30 seconds):**
> Redstick is a $5M early-stage fund investing in AI and robotics for food
> productivity — the whole value chain, farm to consumer. We write $100K and $250K
> checks at pre-seed through Series A across North America. What we bring beyond
> capital is an operator: our GP ran multi-unit food businesses, so we underwrite the
> way an operator would and we support founders the same way. Thesis clarity, process
> discipline, founder-first support — that's the whole pitch.

**Full narrative (site / Investor Guide / long boilerplate):**
> Food is the largest, oldest industry on earth and one of the least automated. Labor
> is scarce, margins are thin, and the technology to fix it — computer vision,
> robotics, decision automation, better inputs — is finally good enough and cheap
> enough to deploy. Redstick Ventures exists to fund that shift at the earliest stage.
>
> We are deliberately narrow: AI and robotics that make food systems more productive,
> across four vectors — labour productivity, optimization, decision automation, and
> input efficiency. We write $100K and $250K checks into pre-seed through Series A
> companies in the US and Canada.
>
> Our edge is not a bigger balance sheet — it is judgment and support from people who
> have operated. Our GP, Cam Crowder, is a former Tim Hortons franchisee and
> multi-unit operator; co-founder Shane Larisey is a manufacturing and supply-chain
> engineer. We run a disciplined seven-stage diligence process so decisions are made
> on evidence, not hype, and we back the founders we fund like operators, not
> spectators. The name is a nod to Baton Rouge — *le bâton rouge*, the "red stick."

## 2. The three-pillar differentiator

Everything Redstick says about itself reduces to these three. Do not add a fourth.

| Pillar | What it means | Proof point (real, not claimed) |
|---|---|---|
| **Thesis clarity** | One narrow, defensible thesis: AI & robotics for food productivity, farm→consumer, four named vectors. We know exactly what we invest in and what we don't. | The four-vector framework; every deal tagged to one vector or `Off-Thesis`. |
| **Process discipline** | A seven-stage pipeline with hard gates and a weighted five-lens scorecard. Decisions are evidence-based and repeatable, not single-point intuition. | The diligence pipeline in [`../03-diligence-underwriting/`](../03-diligence-underwriting/); every financial figure cites its source. |
| **Founder-first operator support** | An operator GP who has run food businesses supports founders on the problems operators actually hit — hiring, supply chain, unit economics, first customers. | Cam's operating background; Shane's manufacturing/supply-chain diligence. |

**Why three and not more:** a fund this size wins on focus. Three pillars are
memorable, mutually reinforcing (clarity → discipline → support), and each is
independently true. A longer list would dilute the operator story, which is the brand.

## 3. Founder ICP — who should pitch Redstick

**Strong fit:**
- Building AI or robotics that makes some part of the food value chain more
  productive (farm, processing, distribution, food service, or consumer).
- Pre-seed to Series A, raising a round where a $100K–$250K check is useful — either
  as an early check or alongside a lead.
- Based in the US or Canada.
- At least one founder with real domain experience in food, ag, or the relevant
  hard tech; can articulate the customer's problem in the customer's words.
- Wants a hands-on early investor who has operated, not a passive logo.

**Poor fit (redirect politely):**
- Pure consumer CPG / food brands with no productivity-technology core.
- Deep biotech / novel-food with a decade-long regulatory path and no near-term
  productivity wedge.
- Companies whose minimum check is well above $250K and who need a lead writing $1M+.
- Anything outside North America, or off-thesis "platform for everything" plays.

> The ICP is a filter, not a wall. Off-thesis founders still get a fast, respectful
> "not for us, here's who might be" — that reputation is itself a sourcing asset.

## 4. Competitive positioning — why a founder picks Redstick

**vs. generalist seed funds (the "we do everything" fund):**

| | Generalist seed fund | Redstick |
|---|---|---|
| Thesis | Broad; food/ag is one of many bets | Only food productivity — we live in the sector |
| Diligence | Fast, pattern-matched | Sector-specific, operator-underwritten |
| Support | Network + general advice | Operator support on food-specific problems |
| Founder takeaway | "One of 200 portfolio companies" | "An investor who understands my business" |

**vs. large agrifood / food-tech funds:**

| | Big agrifood fund | Redstick |
|---|---|---|
| Stage | Series A+ and later; larger checks | Earliest stage — pre-seed/seed concentration |
| Speed | Committee-heavy, slower | Small team, disciplined but fast |
| Attention | Founder is small on a big cap table | Founder gets direct GP time |
| Check | $1M–$5M+ | $100K/$250K — sized to be useful early |

**The one-line competitive answer:** *bigger funds bring more money later; generalists
bring breadth. Redstick brings the earliest-stage combination of a narrow food thesis,
disciplined process, and an operator who has actually run the business.*

## 6. Brand voice & visual notes

**Voice — disciplined operator:**
- Plain, specific, concrete. Prefer a named number over an adjective.
- Confident without hype. We don't say "revolutionary"; we say what the technology does.
- Founder-respecting. Even a pass is direct and useful.
- Honest about stage and size. $5M fund, small checks — stated plainly, framed as focus.
- Never fabricate track record, metrics, or awards. Illustrative examples are labeled
  illustrative.

**Visual:**
- **Primary color: Redstick green `#1C3A2E`** — one disciplined brand color; used for
  headers, accents, and document chrome. Alternating light row `#F2F8F5` in tables.
- Clean, document-forward, operator-serious — not a flashy consumer look.
- The "red stick" / Baton Rouge heritage is a story element, used sparingly, not a
  loud visual motif.
- Typography and layout stay legible and understated; the content carries the weight.
