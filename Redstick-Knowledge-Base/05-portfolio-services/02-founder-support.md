# 05.02 — Founder Support

This is the actual product Redstick sells. A founder can raise $100K or $250K from
almost anyone; the reason to take it from us is the operating help attached to it.
Redstick's differentiator is founder-first support at the earliest stage — real
operational expertise, not just capital. This document is the menu of that help, the
rules for what we lean into vs. politely decline, how founders get it, and how we
ration our time so the breakouts get the most.

## Where our edge actually is

Our value-add is credible only where we have operated. Cam ran multi-unit food
service as a Tim Hortons franchisee and multi-unit operator; Shane is a
manufacturing and supply-chain engineer. We are strongest at the messy, tactical,
operator-side of a food company — the parts a generalist fund cannot help with.

| Support area | Who leads | What it looks like in practice |
|---|---|---|
| **Unit economics** | Cam | Tear down the per-unit / per-site / per-order P&L; find the real contribution margin; pressure-test payback and what has to be true for the model to work at scale. |
| **Franchising & multi-unit ops** | Cam | Site rollout sequencing, labor scheduling, throughput, standardizing operations across locations, franchise-vs-owned economics. |
| **Food supply chain & manufacturing** | Shane | Sourcing, contract manufacturing, quality/QC, scaling production, cost-down engineering, supplier diligence. |
| **GTM to food buyers** | Cam | Selling into restaurants, QSR/multi-unit operators, grocery, and foodservice distributors — who actually signs, procurement cycles, pilot-to-rollout motion, what a buyer needs to say yes. |
| **Hiring (early operating roles)** | Cam/Shane | First ops, sales, and production hires; role scoping; sniff-testing candidates who have run food/industrial operations. |
| **Follow-on fundraise prep** | Cam | Next-round narrative, metrics that matter to the next investor, data-room readiness, target-investor mapping, warm intros to co-investors. |
| **Intros** | Cam/Shane | Customers, hires, co-investors, corporate partners, and policy-capital/agrifood-network contacts where we genuinely know the person. |

**Intros rule:** we only make intros where we actually know the person and believe
the match is real. A low-conviction intro spends our credibility with the recipient
and teaches the founder our intros are noise. Better to say "I don't have the right
person for that" than to forward a cold name.

## Support-request triage

Not every ask is one we should take. We lean into problems where our operator
experience compounds the outcome, and we decline — politely, with a redirect —
problems where we would just be a slow, expensive generalist.

| We lean in on | We politely decline / redirect |
|---|---|
| Unit-economics teardown and scale modeling | Deep legal drafting (redirect to fund/portfolio counsel) |
| Selling into food/restaurant/grocery buyers | Tax structuring and detailed accounting (redirect to accountants) |
| Supply-chain, sourcing, contract-manufacturing (Shane) | Highly technical ML/hardware architecture (we diligence it; we don't build it) |
| First operating/production/sales hires | Generic startup advice already covered by accelerators |
| Next-round story, metrics, data-room prep, co-investor intros | PR/marketing agency selection |
| Multi-unit / franchise rollout strategy | Personal-cofounder-dispute mediation beyond a candid conversation |
| Crisis triage when a company is off the rails | Being an unpaid outsourced executive (we advise; we don't run the company) |

**How to decline well:** name why it's outside our edge, and point to someone or
something better. "That's not where we add value, but here's who is" keeps trust
higher than a vague yes that never delivers.

## The office-hours / on-demand model

Founders reach our help two ways:

1. **Standing office hours.** Cam holds a recurring weekly slot; Shane holds a
   standing supply-chain/manufacturing slot bookable on demand. Founders grab time
   through Olli. Good for: working sessions, "help me think through X," reviewing a
   model or a pitch, hiring debriefs.
2. **On-demand / async.** Anything urgent or quick goes straight to Cam by
   message/email. Our commitment: **respond within 2 business days** (faster for the
   Core tier and for anything flagged crisis). Good for: fast intros, a gut-check
   before a big decision, "is this term normal," escalating a problem.

Founders are told plainly at onboarding: **ask early and be specific.** A vague
"can we chat" gets scheduled; "I need to sanity-check my per-site P&L before an
investor call Thursday" gets an answer today. The monthly-update "Asks" field
(see [`01-onboarding.md`](./01-onboarding.md)) is the standing on-ramp.

## Tying support intensity to the power law

We are a small team supporting 15–20 companies. We cannot give every company the same
hours, and we shouldn't — venture returns are power-law distributed, so support time
should be too. This is a direct consequence of the construction model in
[`../04-portfolio-construction/`](../04-portfolio-construction/): the companies that
can return the fund, and the companies whose failure we might still salvage, earn the
most of our attention.

Every company is assigned a support tier at the end of onboarding (Week 4) and
re-assessed quarterly.

| Tier | Who's in it | Support intensity | Illustrative time split |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Core** | Breakout candidates — the potential fund-returners, and companies actively fundraising a strong up-round | High: proactive, hands-on, priority on intros and fundraise prep, faster response | ~60% of our support hours |
| **Standard** | On-plan companies executing steadily; no breakout signal yet, no crisis | Baseline: monthly update reviewed, office hours available, intros on request | ~25% of our support hours |
| **Watch** | Underperforming or off-plan companies — see the escalation ladder in [`03-board-governance.md`](./03-board-governance.md) | Situational: heavy when there's a fixable crisis, minimal when the outcome is set | ~15% of our support hours |

Notes on using the tiers honestly:

- **Tier is about potential and moment, not loyalty.** A Standard company that starts
  showing breakout signal moves to Core. A Core company that stalls moves back.
- **Watch is not abandonment.** It means we spend heavily only where our help can
  change the outcome (crisis triage, a bridge, a soft landing), and we do not pour
  hours into a company whose fate is already decided. Crisis handling runs on the
  [`../09-playbooks/`](../09-playbooks/) crisis playbook.
- **The power law cuts both ways.** Over-serving a nice-but-going-nowhere company is
  a real cost — it's time not spent on a potential fund-returner. Quarterly review
  exists to catch and correct that drift.

## What good support looks like (and what it doesn't)

| Good | Bad |
|---|---|
| Specific, tactical help from experience we actually have | Generic advice a founder could get from a blog |
| Honest "that's outside our edge, here's who's better" | A polite yes that never turns into anything |
| High-conviction intros to people we know | Cold-forward intros that burn our credibility |
| Proactive outreach to Core companies before they ask | Going quiet until the next fundraise |
| Spending most hours where they change the outcome | Equal time to every company regardless of stage/potential |
