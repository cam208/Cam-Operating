# 05.03 — Board & Governance

Redstick writes $100K or $250K checks. At that size we are almost never the lead and
almost never the largest holder, so we do not take a board *seat* by default. What we
take is **information rights** and, where it's warranted, a **board observer** seat.
This document defines when we take which, what our information rights actually are,
how to be a genuinely useful investor without a vote, how we escalate when a company
underperforms, and how we signal on pro-rata and reserves.

> Canonical facts live in [`../../CLAUDE.md`](../../CLAUDE.md). Where anything here
> disagrees with that file, that file wins. All examples are **illustrative** — no
> real portfolio names or metrics.

---

## Why we don't take board seats by default

This is a deliberate position, consistent with the terms captured in the
[deal-triage skill](../../skills/deal-triage/SKILL.md) (Section 2 — Deal Structure,
where Information Rights and Board Observer are tracked as standard, and a board seat
is not).

- **Our check doesn't earn it.** Board seats belong to the round's lead and the
  larger holders. Asking for a seat off a $100K–$250K check is over-reaching and reads
  as such to a good founder.
- **Board seats carry fiduciary and legal weight.** A seat is a formal director duty,
  personal exposure, and a real time cost. Across 15–20 companies that does not scale
  for a two-person team.
- **We add more value out of the boardroom than in it.** Our edge is tactical operator
  help (see [`02-founder-support.md`](./02-founder-support.md)), which happens in
  working sessions, not in a quarterly governance meeting.
- **Observer + info rights gets us what we need.** Visibility to help and to protect
  the position — without the seat's cost or the founder-relationship friction.

## Observer vs. seat — the decision

Default is **information rights only**. We step up to **observer** when the situation
warrants closer visibility. We take a **seat** only in the rare case it's genuinely
right and offered.

| Signal | Info rights only | Board observer | Board seat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Our check is $100K, we're a small co-investor | ✅ Default | — | — |
| Our check is $250K and we're one of the larger early holders | Possible | ✅ Often | — |
| We're leading or co-leading a pre-seed round | — | ✅ | Rare |
| Founder wants Cam close because of the operator fit | — | ✅ | — |
| Company is a Core-tier breakout we're heavily supporting | — | ✅ | — |
| No lead exists and someone credible needs to hold governance | — | Consider | Rare, only if offered and resourced |
| We'd be taking a seat mainly for signaling/ego | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ Never |

**Rules of thumb:**

- **Never trade away economics for a seat.** A seat is not worth a worse cap or lost
  pro-rata.
- **Observer is the ceiling in most rounds.** If we're pushing past observer, the
  question is whether we should have led — and if we didn't lead, we probably
  shouldn't have the seat.
- **A seat must be resourced.** If we take one, one named partner (usually Cam) owns
  it, prepares for every meeting, and it counts against our support-time budget.
- **Get the observer right in the docs.** Board-observer status and information rights
  go in the side letter at closing — captured during onboarding Week 2 (see
  [`01-onboarding.md`](./01-onboarding.md)). Don't rely on a handshake.

## Information rights — what we expect

Our standard information-rights package, requested in the side letter:

| Right | Expectation |
|---|---|
| Monthly / quarterly updates | Written update with the core metrics (mirrors our monthly template) |
| Annual financials | Year-end statements within a reasonable window |
| Cap table access | Current cap table on request, and after any financing |
| Material-event notice | Prompt notice of financings, key-exec changes, major contracts, and adverse events |
| Budget / plan visibility | Annual operating plan or budget when one exists |
| Pro-rata information | Timely notice and information ahead of any round so we can exercise pro-rata (see below) |

Information rights are a floor, not a leash. If a founder is sending a good monthly
update and picking up the phone, we do not also demand a formal quarterly financials
pack for its own sake. The point is to stay informed enough to help and to protect
the position — not to generate paperwork.

## How to be a useful non-board investor

Not having a vote is not a limitation on being useful — often the reverse. The best
early investors influence through help and candor, not through board control.

- **Show up between meetings.** Value gets added in working sessions and quick asks,
  not in the quarterly cadence. Be the investor the founder calls first.
- **Give an honest read, not a governance vote.** A founder wants "here's what I'd do
  and why," delivered as a peer, not a director's formal position.
- **Be the pre-meeting ally.** Help the founder prep for their real board — sharpen
  the narrative, pressure-test the numbers — so they walk in strong.
- **Respect the lead's role.** Don't undercut the lead investor or the board. Add a
  distinct operator perspective; don't relitigate governance decisions we don't own.
- **Protect the relationship and the info.** Confidentiality is absolute. Nothing a
  founder tells us gets shopped, and we never leak between portfolio companies.
- **Keep observer meetings earning their keep.** If we hold an observer seat, come
  prepared, read the pack in advance, and contribute an operator angle — or hand the
  seat back.

## Escalation when a company underperforms

Underperformance is normal in a pre-seed/seed portfolio — the bear-case floor is
≥ 20% fail probability on every deal (see [`../../CLAUDE.md`](../../CLAUDE.md)). The
job is to catch trouble early and respond proportionately, not to panic or to look
away. This ladder ties into the crisis-management playbook in
[`../09-playbooks/`](../09-playbooks/).

| Level | Trigger (illustrative) | Redstick response | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0 — Normal** | On or near plan; updates on time | Standard cadence; support on request | Olli/Cam |
| **1 — Flag** | One or two soft metrics; a missed or thin update | Olli flags in the Portfolio Snapshot; Cam reaches out to understand it | Olli → Cam |
| **2 — Watch** | Sustained miss, burn ahead of plan, or two updates missed | Move to Watch tier; Cam has a direct, candid conversation; agree corrective actions and a check-in date | Cam |
| **3 — Intervene** | Runway < ~6 months with no plan, key departure, covenant/legal issue, or going-quiet | Hands-on: help on the raise/bridge, hard cost decisions, or a soft landing. Invoke the [`../09-playbooks/`](../09-playbooks/) crisis playbook. Loop Shane if supply-chain is the failure point | Cam + Shane |
| **4 — Resolve** | Wind-down, acqui-hire, or a fundable turnaround | Support the outcome that best protects the fund and treats the founder fairly; mark the position; record for lessons-learned | Cam |

Principles running through the ladder:

- **Bad news early is a founder doing it right.** We never punish honesty in a
  report; we punish being surprised. The onboarding compact makes this explicit.
- **Match effort to salvage value.** Heavy hands-on help when the outcome is still
  movable; a clean, fair wind-down when it isn't. This is the Watch-tier logic from
  [`02-founder-support.md`](./02-founder-support.md).
- **Mark honestly and feed reporting.** Markdowns and write-offs flow to LP reporting
  in [`../06-lp-relations-fund-ops/`](../06-lp-relations-fund-ops/); never carry a
  dead company at cost.
- **Protect the relationship even in failure.** A founder we treated well in a
  wind-down is a founder who comes back to us with their next company.

## Pro-rata & reserve signaling

Pro-rata rights (tracked in the deal terms) let us defend or grow our position in a
company's next round. Whether we exercise is a construction decision governed by
[`../04-portfolio-construction/`](../04-portfolio-construction/) reserves — and we
send a clear, early signal either way.

| Situation | Our signal | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Core-tier breakout raising a strong round | Intent to exercise pro-rata, early and clearly | Concentrate reserves into winners — power-law logic |
| Standard company raising on plan | Case-by-case; decide against reserve availability | Follow-on is earned, not automatic |
| Watch/underperforming company | Usually decline pro-rata; say so kindly and early | Don't throw good money after a set outcome |
| We're tapped out of reserves | Tell the founder straight; help find who can fill the allocation | Honesty protects the relationship and the round |

Signaling discipline:

- **Reserves are never promised at investment.** The onboarding compact says so.
  Pro-rata is a *right*, not a Redstick *commitment* to invest.
- **Signal early.** A founder needs to know if we're in or out well before the round
  closes so they can fill the allocation. Late ambiguity is a disservice.
- **Watch the signaling effect.** Declining pro-rata in an otherwise fine company can
  read as a negative to the new lead. Where we're passing for portfolio-construction
  reasons rather than conviction reasons, say that plainly so it isn't misread.
- **A follow-on is a fresh underwrite.** Any material follow-on runs back through the
  [deal-triage skill](../../skills/deal-triage/SKILL.md) logic at the new terms — we
  don't reflexively double down.
