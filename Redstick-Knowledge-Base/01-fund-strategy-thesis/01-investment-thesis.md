# Investment Thesis

## One sentence

Redstick backs **AI and robotics that make food systems more productive** — across
the entire value chain, from farm to consumer.

## The belief

Food is the largest, oldest, and least-digitized major industry on earth. It runs on
thin margins, chronic and worsening labor shortages, brittle supply chains, and
enormous waste. For the first time, two technology curves — capable, affordable
robotics and production-grade AI (vision, forecasting, decision automation) — are
mature enough to attack those problems at the point of physical work, not just in
back-office software.

The winners will not be the flashiest models. They will be teams that understand the
grinding operational reality of food — the margins, the labor, the perishability, the
buyers — and deploy technology that survives contact with a processing plant, a farm,
or a restaurant. **That operator judgment is Redstick's edge.** Cam Crowder ran
multi-unit food operations (Tim Hortons); Shane Larisey engineered manufacturing and
supply chains. We can tell the difference between a demo and a deployable system.

## The four technology vectors

Every deal is tagged to exactly one vector (or `Off-Thesis`). A company can touch
several; we tag it by where its defensibility and value creation concentrate.

| Vector | What it is | Example problem it attacks |
|---|---|---|
| **Labour Productivity (Robotics)** | Physical systems replacing manual labor in production, processing, and service | Harvesting, deboning, packing, back-of-house food prep, cleaning |
| **Optimization (CV/Sensing)** | AI vision and sensing for grading, sorting, QC, yield | Produce grading, foreign-object detection, ripeness/quality sorting |
| **Decision Automation (AI/Ops)** | AI automating forecasting, inventory, scheduling, compliance | Demand forecasting, dynamic ordering, labor scheduling, food-safety compliance |
| **Input Efficiency (Bio/Materials)** | Novel materials/bioprocesses improving production efficiency | Alternative inputs, yield-boosting bioprocesses, waste-to-value, shelf-life |

## What "on-thesis" means

A deal is on-thesis when **all** of the following hold:

1. The core value creation is one of the four vectors above.
2. The customer is somewhere on the food value chain (farm, input supplier, processor,
   distributor, grocer, restaurant, or the end consumer's food experience).
3. The technology makes the food system **more productive** — more output per unit of
   labor, capital, land, or input; less waste; better quality — not merely a nicer UI
   on an existing food business.

## What is off-thesis (and why we still see it)

- **Food brands / CPG products** — a great oat-milk brand is not a productivity
  technology. Off-thesis.
- **Pure restaurant/ghost-kitchen operators** — operating a food business ≠ making the
  system more productive with technology.
- **Generic horizontal AI/SaaS** with a food logo on the landing page but no food-
  specific defensibility.
- **Ag-biotech with a >18-month regulatory path to launch** — may be a great company,
  wrong stage/risk profile for a $5M seed fund (see kill criteria in
  [`../03-diligence-underwriting/`](../03-diligence-underwriting/)).

We still take the meeting when a warm operator sends it, but off-thesis deals are
declined quickly and kindly.

## Why this thesis wins for Redstick specifically

- **Non-consensus but proven-adjacent.** Food-productivity tech is under-capitalized
  relative to its TAM versus generic AI. Less competition on price at entry.
- **Operator credibility compounds sourcing.** Founders in food want investors who have
  actually run food operations. That gets us into rooms generalist seed funds can't.
- **Diligence advantage.** We can pressure-test supply chain, unit economics, and buyer
  behavior faster and more accurately than a generalist, lowering our loss rate.

## Thesis maintenance

The thesis is reviewed **quarterly** against what the pipeline and portfolio actually
show, and **fully** once a year. Vectors can be re-weighted, but the core belief —
technology that makes food physically more productive — is the fund's identity. See
[`../../operating-cadence.md`](../../operating-cadence.md).
