# 04 — Network Strategy

Sourcing quality is downstream of relationships. This document is the deliberate plan
for building the network that produces high-signal deal flow, co-investment, diligence
access, and — eventually — LP and exit relationships. For a $5M Fund I, the network is
the single biggest non-capital asset the firm has.

> The network exists to raise our **quality-inbound rate** and our **conversion at every
> stage** — the two levers the [funnel math](./03-funnel-math.md) shows matter most.
> Fund facts: [`../../CLAUDE.md`](../../CLAUDE.md).

---

## The operating premise: give-to-get

Redstick is a small, new fund. We cannot win relationships with capital or brand — we
win them by being **useful first**. Every relationship in this document runs on a
give-to-get basis: we lead with value (operator expertise, honest diligence, real
introductions, closed loops), and deal flow follows as a byproduct of being a good
node in the ecosystem.

**What Redstick uniquely gives:**

- **Operator judgment.** Cam has actually run food/restaurant operations (Tim Hortons
  franchisee, multi-unit operator). Shane has run manufacturing and supply chains. In a
  sector full of technologists, ground-truth on whether a robot survives a real kitchen
  or a real field is scarce and valuable — to founders, co-investors, and corporates
  alike.
- **Thesis-native diligence.** We can pressure-test food-productivity claims fast and
  credibly. Co-investors and accelerators value a partner who does real work.
- **Closed loops.** We tell every introducer what happened. This alone makes us a
  preferred node — most funds go dark.
- **Demand-side reach.** Through FCC/CFIN and operator relationships, we can connect
  portfolio companies to actual farms, processors, and food-service buyers.

## Reciprocity in practice (the give-to-get loop)

The mechanism that keeps every relationship alive:

1. **Lead with a give.** Before asking for deal flow, deliver something: a founder intro,
   an operator take on a company they're diligencing, a portfolio company that fits their
   thesis, an hour of office hours for a cohort.
2. **Make asks specific.** "Send me AI/robotics-for-food-productivity companies at
   pre-seed–Series A in North America, $100K–$250K checks" beats "send me deals." A
   thesis a contact can hold in their head is a filter working for us for free.
3. **Always close the loop.** Every intro gets an outcome told back — advanced, passed
   (with the one-line reason), or watching. This is non-negotiable and is our single
   cheapest reputation-builder.
4. **Share, don't hoard.** When we see a deal that fits a co-investor better than us, we
   send it. Generous nodes get sent deals back. Hoarding kills a small fund's network.
5. **Be a reliable syndicate partner.** Do real diligence, communicate clearly, don't
   renege. Co-investors remember behavior in a live deal far more than any pitch.

## Value we add to the ecosystem (positioning)

Redstick's ecosystem brand is **"the operator-savvy capital for food-productivity
tech."** Concretely, we want the ecosystem to associate us with:

- **Ground-truth diligence** — the fund that knows whether the technology survives
  contact with a real food operation.
- **Founder-useful capital** — operator help with unit economics, supply chain, and
  food-industry go-to-market (see [`../05-portfolio-services/`](../05-portfolio-services/)).
- **Thesis clarity** — everyone in our network can state what we invest in, which makes
  them better scouts for us.
- **Reliability** — we close loops, we don't waste founders' or partners' time, and we
  behave well in syndicates.

## Cadence — keeping the network warm

Relationships decay without contact. The maintenance rhythm:

| Cadence | Action | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| **Continuous** | Close every intro loop; lead with gives; log touches | Cam / Olli |
| **Monthly** | Touch a rotating slice of Tier 1 operators (each hit ≥ quarterly) | Cam |
| **Per event** | Work THRIVE/SVG, CFIN, accelerator demo days deliberately, with follow-ups within 48h | Cam / Shane |
| **Quarterly** | Full relationship review (below) | Cam + Shane |
| **Annually** | Re-map the network vs. the fund's needs; prune and add target relationships | Cam |

---

## Quarterly relationship review

A standing 45–60 minute review, run alongside the quarterly funnel review in
[`03-funnel-math.md`](./03-funnel-math.md). Owners: **Cam + Shane**. Output: an updated
target relationship map and a short list of next-quarter moves.

**Agenda:**

1. **Attribution.** Which relationships produced deal flow this quarter — and which
   produced deals that *advanced*, not just arrived? Attribution feeds
   [`01-sourcing-channels.md`](./01-sourcing-channels.md).
2. **Give/get balance.** For each key relationship: are we net-giving or net-taking?
   Net-takers go cold. Identify where we owe a give.
3. **Coverage gaps.** Which of the five relationship classes is thin? (Early on,
   co-investors and corporates typically need the most building.)
4. **Warmth check.** Any Tier 1 relationship not touched in a quarter is a flag —
   schedule contact.
5. **Loop audit.** Any open intro loops not yet closed? Close them this week.
6. **Next-quarter targets.** Name 3–5 specific new relationships to build and the give
   that opens each one.

**A healthy network looks like:** every relationship class has at least a few deep
contacts · we are net-giving to our most important nodes · no Tier 1 relationship has
gone cold · a majority of *advanced* deals trace back to a named relationship, not cold
inbound.

## Anti-patterns

- **Transactional asking.** Reaching out only when we want something. The network
  remembers.
- **Open loops.** Taking an intro and never reporting back — the fastest way to make a
  scout stop scouting.
- **Breadth over depth.** A thousand LinkedIn connections is not a network. Ten people
  who think of us when they meet an on-thesis founder is.
- **Hoarding deals.** Refusing to share deals we can't lead. Small funds win by being
  generous nodes.
- **Neglecting the demand side.** Corporate and FCC/CFIN relationships aren't just deal
  channels — they're how our portfolio companies find customers. Undervaluing them
  starves the portfolio of go-to-market help.
