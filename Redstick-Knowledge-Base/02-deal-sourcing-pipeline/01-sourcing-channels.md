# 01 — Sourcing Channels

Where Redstick deal flow comes from, ranked by signal. The governing principle:
**a small number of high-signal, relationship-driven channels will produce most of
the portfolio; the rest is filtered volume.** We would rather see 40 on-thesis deals
from people who know our thesis than 400 cold decks.

> Fund facts are canonical in [`../../CLAUDE.md`](../../CLAUDE.md). Thesis vectors and
> the on-thesis test come from there and from the
> [deal-triage skill](../../skills/deal-triage/SKILL.md).

---

## The signal hierarchy

Not all deal flow is equal. We sort every channel into three tiers by the quality of
the deals it produces and how well the source understands our thesis:

- **Tier 1 — Highest signal (relationship-driven):** warm operator/founder intros,
  the SVG/THRIVE agrifood network, portfolio referrals. The source knows us, knows
  the thesis, and pre-filters. Highest thesis-fit rate, lowest volume.
- **Tier 2 — Structured signal (curated lists & institutions):** RBCx "Companies
  Raising," CFIN, FCC, accelerators. Curated but not thesis-specific; good volume,
  moderate fit.
- **Tier 3 — Raw volume (inbound):** website inbound and cold outreach. Highest
  volume, lowest fit, but the cheapest to filter with triage and occasionally a gem.

**Target mix over the fund:** roughly 55% of *invested* deals should originate from
Tier 1, ~30% from Tier 2, ~15% from Tier 3 — even though Tier 3 is the largest by raw
count. If Tier 1 is not producing, that is the single most important sourcing problem
to fix, because those deals convert best.

## The channels

### 1. Warm operator / founder intros  — TIER 1

**What it is.** Introductions from people who have actually run food, ag, restaurant,
or manufacturing operations, and from founders (ours and others') who vouch for a
company. Cam's Tim Hortons / multi-unit operating background and Shane's supply-chain
engineering network are the core asset here.

**How Redstick works it.** Cam maintains a named list of ~25–40 operators, ex-
colleagues, and founder friends and stays top-of-mind so they think of us when they
meet a relevant company. Every intro gets a same-day acknowledgment and a real
outcome told back to the introducer ("we passed because X" / "we're taking a call").
Operators are treated as scouts, not just contacts — we tell them exactly what
on-thesis looks like (the four vectors) so they filter for us.

**Expected quality / volume.** Low volume, highest thesis-fit and highest conviction.
An intro from a trusted operator who understands the problem is the strongest possible
top-of-funnel signal.

**Owner.** Cam. **Cadence.** Continuous; each active operator relationship touched at
least quarterly (see [`04-network-strategy.md`](./04-network-strategy.md)).

---

### 2. SVG / THRIVE agrifood network  — TIER 1

**What it is.** SVG Ventures / THRIVE is the leading agrifood-tech accelerator and
investor network globally — programs, corporate partners, and a dense founder
community squarely on our thesis. This is the single best *thesis-matched* external
network available to us.

**How Redstick works it.** We are an active participant, not a logo on a list: attend
demo days and network events, maintain relationships with program managers, and offer
operator diligence help on food/robotics deals so we see companies early. Because the
network is thesis-native, its referrals arrive pre-sorted into something close to our
four vectors.

**Expected quality / volume.** Moderate volume, very high thesis-fit — the best
volume-to-fit trade in the whole channel set. Also a primary co-investor sourcing pool.

**Owner.** Cam (relationship), Shane (technical diligence support offered into the
network). **Cadence.** Continuous; formal relationship review quarterly.

---

### 3. Portfolio referrals  — TIER 1

**What it is.** Our own founders introducing us to other founders — the highest-trust
channel a fund can build, because a founder who had a good experience with us is the
most credible possible advocate.

**How Redstick works it.** We ask, explicitly and at the right moment (post-close, and
again after we have delivered real help). We make it easy: a founder should be able to
forward one clean paragraph on what we invest in. Referrals are logged with the
referring company named so we can thank them and close the loop.

**Expected quality / volume.** Very low volume early (the portfolio is only ~1/3
deployed), rising over the fund's life. Highest fit and trust. A compounding asset.

**Owner.** Cam. **Cadence.** Triggered post-close and after every material portfolio
win; reviewed quarterly.

---

### 4. RBCx "Companies Raising" lists  — TIER 2

**What it is.** RBCx (RBC's tech-and-innovation arm) circulates curated lists of
Canadian companies actively raising. Broad across sectors; a reliable, steady feed of
companies at exactly the moment they need capital.

**How Redstick works it.** Olli screens each list against the four vectors and the
stage/geography box, pulls the on-thesis names, and does a quick surface check before
Cam decides whether to reach out. Because these companies are actively raising, timing
is good, but fit must be filtered hard — most names will be off-thesis.

**Expected quality / volume.** Good volume, moderate thesis-fit after filtering. Strong
Canadian coverage and strategic-LP-fit signal (Canadian founders → FCC/CFIN relevance).

**Owner.** Olli (screen), Cam (outreach decision). **Cadence.** Per list release
(roughly monthly); screened within a few days of receipt.

---

### 5. CFIN — Canadian Food Innovation Network  — TIER 2

**What it is.** A national organization connecting food producers, processors, and
tech companies, with funding programs and a member network. Directly on our thesis
(food productivity) and a bridge to Canadian food-industry incumbents and policy
capital.

**How Redstick works it.** Membership-level engagement: monitor funded projects and
member companies, attend events, and position Redstick as the operator-savvy capital
partner for CFIN-backed food-tech startups. CFIN also matters for *strategic LP fit* —
Canadian, food-productivity, regional-development signal that we track for reporting.

**Expected quality / volume.** Moderate volume, high thesis-fit (food-specific), strong
Canadian/policy-capital angle. Some companies are grant-stage / pre-venture — filter for
venture-scale ambition.

**Owner.** Cam. **Cadence.** Continuous membership engagement; event-driven bursts.

---

### 6. FCC — Farm Credit Canada  — TIER 2

**What it is.** Canada's largest agriculture-and-food lender, with deep reach into farm
and agribusiness operators and its own venture/innovation activity. A relationship-and-
policy-capital channel more than a raw deal feed.

**How Redstick works it.** We build the relationship for co-investment, farm-operator
introductions (ground-truth on whether a robot actually works in the field), and
policy-capital alignment. FCC's network is the demand side of our thesis — the farms and
processors our companies must sell to — so it doubles as diligence access.

**Expected quality / volume.** Low direct deal volume, high strategic value (validation,
co-invest, LP-fit). Treat as a network relationship first, a deal channel second — see
[`04-network-strategy.md`](./04-network-strategy.md).

**Owner.** Cam. **Cadence.** Relationship-driven; reviewed quarterly.

---

### 7. Accelerators & programs  — TIER 2

**What it is.** Agrifood and deep-tech accelerators beyond THRIVE — regional programs,
university spinout programs, robotics/hardware accelerators. Demo days concentrate a
cohort of raising companies at a known moment.

**How Redstick works it.** Prioritize agrifood- and robotics-specific programs over
generalist ones (higher fit). Get on demo-day invite lists, track cohorts against the
thesis, and reach out pre-demo-day where a company is clearly on-thesis. Generalist
accelerator output is filtered as Tier-3-grade volume.

**Expected quality / volume.** Bursty (cohort-timed), moderate fit for thesis-specific
programs, low for generalist. Good for meeting technical co-founders early.

**Owner.** Olli (cohort screening), Cam (relationships with program leads). **Cadence.**
Aligned to demo-day calendar (typically quarterly per program).

---

### 8. Website / inbound  — TIER 3

**What it is.** Founders submitting through the Redstick site or emailing cold. Highest
volume, lowest average fit, but cheap to filter and the front door for founders who
found us through our content/positioning.

**How Redstick works it.** Everything goes into the pipeline. Olli triages any
*complete* package within 48h (see the SLA in
[`02-pipeline-crm-ops.md`](./02-pipeline-crm-ops.md)); incomplete submissions get a
templated request for the missing pieces. A clear, thesis-specific website ("we back AI
& robotics for food productivity, farm to consumer, $100K–$250K checks") is the cheapest
way to raise inbound fit — self-selection does the first filter for us.

**Expected quality / volume.** Highest volume, lowest thesis-fit rate. Occasionally
produces a strong on-thesis deal, which justifies keeping the intake disciplined rather
than closing it.

**Owner.** Olli. **Cadence.** Continuous intake; 48h triage SLA on complete packages.

## Channel scorecard

Relative ratings, calibrated for a $5M Fund I. "Thesis-fit rate" = share of deals from
that channel that are genuinely on-thesis and stage/geography-appropriate. "Effort" =
ongoing Redstick effort to keep the channel productive. Volume is *relative*, not a
promise of specific counts.

| Channel | Tier | Volume | Thesis-fit rate | Effort | Primary owner | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Warm operator / founder intros | 1 | Low | Very high | High | Cam | Highest conviction; our core edge |
| SVG / THRIVE network | 1 | Medium | Very high | Medium | Cam / Shane | Best volume-to-fit trade; also co-invest pool |
| Portfolio referrals | 1 | Low→rising | Very high | Low | Cam | Compounds as portfolio grows |
| RBCx "Companies Raising" | 2 | High | Moderate | Low | Olli / Cam | Good timing, strong CDN coverage |
| CFIN | 2 | Medium | High | Medium | Cam | Food-specific; LP-fit signal |
| FCC | 2 | Low | Moderate | Medium | Cam | Relationship/policy-capital first |
| Accelerators & programs | 2 | Bursty | Mod (high if thesis-specific) | Medium | Olli / Cam | Prioritize agrifood/robotics programs |
| Website / inbound | 3 | Very high | Low | Low | Olli | Filtered by triage; front door |

**Reading the scorecard.** Effort should track signal. We invest the most effort in
Tier 1 because those channels produce the deals that actually convert (see the
conversion assumptions in [`03-funnel-math.md`](./03-funnel-math.md)). Tier 3 is
low-effort by design — we let the website and triage do the filtering rather than
spending Cam's time there.

## Anti-patterns (how sourcing goes wrong)

- **Chasing volume over signal.** More cold decks is not progress. The metric that
  matters is on-thesis, high-conviction deals seen, not total inbound.
- **Letting Tier 1 go cold.** Operator and portfolio relationships decay without
  contact. A quarter with no operator touchpoints is a sourcing failure even if the
  pipeline looks full of Tier 3 volume.
- **Off-thesis drift.** Interesting-but-off-thesis deals (great founder, wrong sector)
  waste diligence time. Triage tags `Off-Thesis` early and hard.
- **Not closing the loop.** Every intro deserves an outcome told back to the introducer.
  This is what keeps Tier 1 channels alive; see the reciprocity model in
  [`04-network-strategy.md`](./04-network-strategy.md).
