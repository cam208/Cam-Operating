# 07.03 — Conflicts of Interest Policy

A real, usable conflicts-of-interest policy for Redstick Ventures. Unlike the other two
documents in this section, this one is about the **GP's own conduct**, not about entity
law — so it is concrete and adoptable as written. It exists because the GP's integrity
is the fund's core asset: LPs trust Cam and Shane to put fund interests first, and
founders trust Redstick with confidential information. This policy keeps that trust
defensible.

> **Nature of this document:** an operational policy the fund can adopt as-is. It is not
> legal advice; fund counsel should review it and reconcile it with the LPA (whose
> conflicts provisions govern if they differ). But it does not depend on any fabricated
> legal fact — it can be put into practice today.

> Canonical fund facts live in [`../../CLAUDE.md`](../../CLAUDE.md).

---

## 1. Purpose & scope

**Purpose.** To identify conflicts of interest, disclose them, and manage them so that
Redstick always acts in the best interest of Fund I and its LPs — and treats founders
fairly and consistently.

**Who this binds** ("Covered Persons"):
- **Cam Crowder** (GP) and **Shane Larisey** (co-founder)
- Any future partners, employees, advisors, or contractors of the GP / management company
- **Olli** (AI EA) in its coordination role — Olli surfaces potential conflicts it
  notices but makes no judgment calls

**What is a conflict?** Any situation where a Covered Person's personal interest, another
fund/vehicle's interest, or a third party's interest could influence — or appear to
influence — a decision that should be made solely in Fund I's and its LPs' interest.
**Appearance counts.** A conflict does not require bad intent or actual harm; a
reasonable LP's perception is enough to require disclosure.

**Guiding principle.** When Fund I's interest and any other interest could diverge, the
default is: **disclose it, document it, and where material, recuse from the decision.**

---

## 2. Deal allocation between vehicles (funds / SPVs)

As Redstick grows it may run SPVs or future funds alongside Fund I. Allocation of a deal
among vehicles is the highest-stakes conflict for a manager — it decides who gets the
good deals.

**Policy:**
- **Fund I has first call on any opportunity within its mandate** (AI & robotics for food
  productivity, pre-seed–Series A, North America, $100K/$250K checks) while Fund I is in
  its investment period and has capacity, unless the LPA or an SPV's terms say otherwise.
- **An opportunity goes to an SPV or other vehicle only when** Fund I cannot or should
  not take the full amount — e.g. the check exceeds Fund I's $100K/$250K discipline, the
  concentration limit is reached, reserves are committed, or the deal is outside Fund I's
  mandate. The *reason* is recorded in the conflicts register.
- **No cherry-picking.** A Covered Person may not route a strong deal to a personal
  vehicle or SPV they benefit from disproportionately while steering weaker deals to
  Fund I. Allocation is decided on the fund's rules, not on who profits.
- **Allocation among multiple eligible vehicles** uses a pre-stated, consistent basis
  (e.g. mandate fit first, then available capacity), documented at the time.

---

## 3. GP personal angel investing vs. the fund

Cam and Shane are operators with their own networks; personal angel activity is normal
and can benefit the fund's dealflow. But it creates a direct conflict with Fund I's first
call on opportunities.

**Policy:**
- **The fund comes first.** A Covered Person may **not** personally invest in a company
  that fits Fund I's mandate and that Fund I would plausibly consider, **unless**:
  1. The opportunity has been offered to Fund I and the IC has declined it (with the
     reason recorded), **or**
  2. Fund I is investing and the personal investment is a pre-existing position disclosed
     before the fund's decision, **and** it does not distort the fund's terms or
     allocation.
- **Pre-existing personal positions** (angel investments made before this policy or
  before the fund considered the company) must be **disclosed** and logged in the
  register the moment the company enters Fund I's pipeline. The conflicted principal
  recuses from the fund's decision on that company (Section 8).
- **No competing with the fund for allocation.** A Covered Person does not take personal
  allocation in a round the fund is investing in if doing so reduces the fund's ability
  to get its intended check or terms.
- **Follow-ons.** Personal follow-on into a fund portfolio company is a related-party
  situation — disclose and recuse (Section 4).

---

## 4. Related-party & affiliated transactions

**Policy:** Any transaction between Fund I and a Covered Person, their family, an entity
they control, or another Redstick vehicle is a **related-party transaction** requiring
heightened handling:

- [ ] **Disclose** the relationship in full before any decision.
- [ ] **Recuse** the conflicted principal from approving it (Section 8).
- [ ] **Arm's-length terms** — the transaction must be on terms no worse to the fund than
      an independent third party would get.
- [ ] **LP consent where the LPA requires it** for affiliated transactions above any
      stated threshold `[confirm with LPA]`.
- [ ] **Log** it in the conflicts register with the terms and the basis for fairness.

Examples: the fund investing in a company a principal already backs; the management
company charging the fund for services; co-investing alongside a principal's SPV; a
principal buying or selling an asset to/from the fund.

## 5. Board & observer conflicts

Redstick takes board or observer seats at portfolio companies (see
[`../05-portfolio-services/`](../05-portfolio-services/)). A director/observer owes duties
to *that company*; the GP owes duties to *the fund*. These can diverge — most sharply in
down rounds, follow-on decisions, acquisitions, and pay-to-play situations.

**Policy:**
- **Name the hat.** When acting as a director/observer, act in the company's interest per
  that role's duties; when deciding the fund's action (invest, follow, sell, vote the
  fund's shares), act in the fund's interest. Do not blur them.
- **Cross-portfolio competition.** A Covered Person may **not** simultaneously sit on the
  boards of two companies that directly compete without disclosure and an explicit,
  logged management plan — and generally Redstick avoids funding direct competitors (see
  information barriers, Section 6).
- **Confidential company information** learned as a director is used for that company's
  benefit and the fund's investment stewardship — never to advantage a competing
  portfolio company or a Covered Person personally.
- **Fund-vs-company conflicts** (e.g. a financing that's good for the fund's position but
  contested by other shareholders) are disclosed to co-investors as appropriate and, if
  material, discussed with counsel.

---

## 6. Information barriers

Redstick sees confidential material from many companies, some of which compete. Handling
that information cleanly is both a conflicts issue and a data-handling issue (see
[`02-regulatory.md`](./02-regulatory.md), founder-data handling).

**Policy:**
- **Do not funnel one company's confidential information to a competitor** — including a
  portfolio company. This is an absolute rule.
- **Avoid funding direct competitors.** If Redstick is already invested in a company, it
  generally will not fund a direct competitor. If a genuine edge case arises, it is
  disclosed to both founders as appropriate, logged, and managed with a defined barrier —
  or declined.
- **Compartmentalize diligence.** Confidential data rooms and triage materials stay with
  the deal team and are not shared laterally across competing evaluations.
- **Founder disclosure.** When Redstick declines a company partly because it conflicts
  with an existing position, it says so honestly at the appropriate level of detail
  rather than stringing the founder along.

---

## 7. Gifts, entertainment & personal benefits

Small emerging managers still need a bright line so favors don't quietly buy influence.

**Policy:**
- **Nominal business courtesies are fine** — a normal meal, a conference ticket, modest
  branded items.
- **Decline or disclose anything that could influence, or appear to influence, a
  decision** — e.g. significant gifts, lavish travel, or anything from a party with a
  live matter before Redstick (a founder mid-diligence, a co-investor negotiating terms).
- **Suggested threshold: disclose and log any single gift/benefit over ~$250, and never
  accept anything conditioned on a decision.** Adjust the number if the team prefers; the
  principle is what matters.
- **No kickbacks, ever** — no personal payment or benefit in exchange for the fund making
  or steering an investment.

---

## 8. Co-investment allocation fairness

When a deal has room for co-investors (LPs, strategics, other funds), allocating that
room is a conflict — it decides who gets access to potentially strong companies.

**Policy:**
- **The fund's own allocation is set first** and is not reduced to create room for
  favored co-investors.
- **Co-invest is offered on a fair, consistent basis** — a pre-stated logic (e.g.
  strategic value to the company, LP relationship, ability to move on the timeline),
  applied consistently and documented, not ad hoc favoritism.
- **A Covered Person's personal co-invest** in a fund deal is a related-party position:
  disclose, recuse from the fund's decision, and do not take personal allocation that
  crowds out the fund (Sections 3–4).
- **Log material co-invest allocations** and the basis for them in the register.

---

## 9. Disclosure & recusal process

The mechanism that makes this policy real.

**Step 1 — Identify & disclose (immediately).** Any Covered Person who becomes aware of
an actual, potential, or apparent conflict discloses it **before** the relevant decision
— to the other principal, in writing (email or a note in the register). Olli flags
anything it notices to Cam; Olli does not adjudicate.

**Step 2 — Assess materiality.** The non-conflicted principal (with counsel if needed)
decides whether the conflict is material to the decision at hand.

**Step 3 — Manage.**
- **Not material** → note it, proceed, log it.
- **Material** → the conflicted principal **recuses** from the decision. The decision is
  made by the non-conflicted principal / IC. Terms must be arm's-length. Where the LPA
  requires it, seek **LP or advisory-committee consent** `[confirm with LPA]`.
- **Cannot be managed** → decline the transaction.

**Step 4 — Document.** Every material conflict — and its resolution — is entered in the
**conflicts register** (Section 10). The register is reviewed by the GP at least
**annually** and whenever a new event is added.

> **Recusal reality check for a two-person firm:** if one principal recuses, the other
> decides. If *both* are conflicted, the fund does not proceed without independent input
> — escalate to counsel and, where applicable, to LPs / an advisory committee. Do not
> let "we're small" become an excuse to skip recusal.

## 11. Policy administration

- **Owner:** Cam (GP), on behalf of the GP entity.
- **Review cadence:** policy reviewed **annually**; register updated **on every event**.
  Aligns with the semi-annual compliance review in
  [`../../operating-cadence.md`](../../operating-cadence.md).
- **Counsel:** fund counsel reviews this policy and reconciles it with the LPA's
  conflicts provisions `[confirm with LPA]`. The LPA governs where they differ.
- **Escalation:** any conflict a Covered Person is unsure how to handle goes to the other
  principal and, if still unclear or material, to counsel — before acting.

---

## Conflicts checklist (quick reference)

- [ ] Conflict disclosed **in writing, before** the decision
- [ ] Materiality assessed by the non-conflicted principal
- [ ] Conflicted principal **recused** where material
- [ ] Terms confirmed **arm's-length** for related-party transactions
- [ ] **LP / advisory-committee consent** obtained where the LPA requires it `[confirm]`
- [ ] Entry added to the **conflicts register** with the management action
- [ ] Fund I's **first-call and allocation** rights protected (no cherry-picking)
- [ ] No confidential information crossed between competing companies
- [ ] Gifts over threshold disclosed; nothing accepted conditioned on a decision
- [ ] Escalated to counsel if novel, material, or both principals conflicted
